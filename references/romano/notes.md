## Content Structure Observations

The intended journey appears to move from proposition and trust signals into self-selection, category fit, and a six-step process. Facility proof and company history deepen confidence before testimonials and educational resources. The lead magnet captures visitors who are not yet ready for an RFQ, while the quote form handles higher-intent buyers. This is a broad conversion funnel with both soft and hard conversion points.

The service-path cards are particularly clear because they distinguish ready-made, custom, and existing-formula needs. The long company journey section adds emotional reassurance but may be more history than many OEM buyers require.

## UI Pattern Observations

The design uses white and very pale blue sections with a bright cyan accent, then switches to deep navy for history, inquiry, and footer content. Cards are consistently rounded with thin borders and moderate whitespace. Category imagery adds warmth, while the dark timeline creates a strong mid-page chapter break. CTAs are direct and repeated at decision points.

The supplied hero area is nearly entirely blank. It must not be treated as an intentional whitespace strategy without reviewing a live or corrected capture. Mobile behavior is unknown.

## Potential Reusable Patterns

- Three service paths based on formula readiness
- Product-category image grid followed by a single consultation CTA
- Six-step concept-to-shelf timeline
- Facility proof with compliance checklist
- Company-history timeline plus compact metrics
- Two-stage conversion: preparation resource before quote form
- Quote section that pairs response expectations with the form

## Things We Should Not Copy

- Facility, product, laboratory, social, and article imagery
- Testimonials, metrics, compliance claims, dates, and response promises
- The exact cyan/navy card system or timeline styling
- The blank hero capture as if it were a deliberate pattern
- Consumer-facing social imagery unrelated to a client's B2B proof strategy
