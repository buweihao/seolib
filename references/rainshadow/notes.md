## Content Structure Observations

The page serves two jobs: a direct wholesale storefront and a manufacturing lead source. It makes the route choice immediately visible, then prioritizes transactional concerns such as financing, categories, and featured products. Manufacturing history and capability appear late and briefly. This works for buyers who already want products, but it leaves procurement and custom-development questions largely unanswered.

The overlapping trust bar is the main bridge between the natural-product hero and the three service paths. The strongest reusable content idea is the early separation of buy-direct, private-label, and custom manufacturing audiences.

## UI Pattern Observations

The visual language is soft and approachable: muted green and pale blue, serif headings, airy white fields, minimal borders, and simple product cutouts. The hero and category tiles use calm, low-contrast color blocks. CTAs are small and transactional. The footer is unusually spacious relative to the compact homepage.

The evidence is desktop-only. Some narrow headings and small controls would need mobile and accessibility review before reuse.

## Potential Reusable Patterns

- Three-route choice between direct stock, private label, and custom development
- Floating trust panel bridging hero and service selection
- Soft pastel product-category mosaic
- Late company-introduction block for a commerce-first journey

## Things We Should Not Copy

- Product photography, packaging, brand marks, certification icons, and promotional pricing
- Pay-later promotion for projects that do not operate an ecommerce checkout
- Exact pastel palette, typography, or overlapping trust-panel design
- Specific GMP, origin, longevity, MOQ, and logistics claims without evidence
- Consumer/storefront emphasis when the client needs a procurement-led B2B site
